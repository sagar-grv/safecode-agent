# Kavach Sentinel product story

## Core idea

**Nothing ships on a guess.** Kavach Sentinel is the proof laboratory for secure software: it turns a vulnerability hypothesis into a bounded patch and then into evidence that the fix holds.

The experience should feel like a quiet mission room rather than a conventional dashboard. The visual world is built from a black field, warm paper, acid-lime verification signals, and a restrained blue evidence trace. Large statements establish the story; small technical modules prove it.

## Narrative arc

The website follows one question: **Can an autonomous system make a security change that deserves trust?** The answer unfolds through five chapters: the brief, the control loop, the case file, the architecture, and the judge brief.

## Page architecture

| Route | Purpose | Primary action |
|---|---|---|
| `/` | Editorial landing page that introduces the product promise and the five-stage story. | Enter the live control room. |
| `/kavach` | Live synthetic control loop with active-stage motion and evidence tabs. | Run the control loop. |
| `/evidence` | Case-file page focused on the BFLA finding with baseline exploit, patch strategy, and proof of fix. | Compare before and after. |
| `/architecture` | System map showing bounded inputs, analysis, structured reasoning, isolated patching, and verifier gate. | Inspect the safety boundary. |
| `/submission` | Judge-oriented brief with competition mapping, demo sequence, metrics caveat, and finale readiness. | Download or present the proposal. |

## Shared visual grammar

The same elements recur on every page: an oversized chapter index, a thin lime calibration line, a small “synthetic demo” badge, a four-stage loop, and a persistent footer stating the sandbox boundary. The website uses high-contrast type, fewer but larger sections, and asymmetrical editorial composition. Motion is functional: chapter index changes, lines draw into the next section, stage markers pulse during a run, and evidence panels crossfade without losing context.

## Deck mapping

The five-slide deck mirrors the site chapters. The cover is the product promise, slide one is the brief, slide two is the control loop, slide three is the architecture, slide four is the evidence and novelty case, and slide five is the judge brief. The four-stage loop stays in the same spatial order across slides so Fade or Morph transitions can connect the story.
